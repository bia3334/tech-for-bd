Infrastructure · Cache & Redis

Why is your system so slow?

It is the question every CIO asks in the second meeting. Nine times out of ten the honest answer is not "a bigger server" — it is a cache. And the cache they will name is Redis.

12 minNo codeEnds with 5 lines you can use in a meeting
01Understand

The archive room and the notepad

A prospect's Customer 360 dashboard takes eight seconds to load. Their IT lead blames "the server". Their current vendor has quoted a hardware upgrade. You are in the room, and you need a better question than "what's the budget?"

Here is what is actually happening. Every time somebody opens that screen, the application walks down to the database and asks the same six questions again: who is this customer, what have they bought, what is their credit limit, what tickets are open. Six questions. Every user. Every single time.

The database answers correctly. It just answers slowly — and it answers the exact same thing over and over.

Tiếng ViệtDatabase = kho hồ sơ trung tâm của doanh nghiệp. Nó trả lời đúng, nhưng chậm, và bị hỏi đi hỏi lại cùng một câu suốt cả ngày.

A database is the archive room downstairs. A cache is the notepad on your desk.

You do not walk down to the archive for a price you looked up thirty seconds ago. You wrote it on the pad. Redis is that notepad — an in-memory store that sits in front of the database and answers repeat questions in about one millisecond instead of a hundred and twenty.

Memory (RAM) is roughly a hundred times faster than disk. That is the entire trick. It is also why Redis costs more per gigabyte, and why it is never the place your customer's real records live.

    05Take away

    Five lines worth stealing

    1. "Is the system slow because it is doing hard work, or because it is doing the same work repeatedly?"
      Opens the technical discussion without pretending to be an architect. Repetition means a cache. Hard work means something else entirely — and that is a different, larger deal.
    2. "Redis does not replace your database. It protects it."
      Defuses the instinct that you are attacking an existing SAP or Oracle investment. You are adding a layer in front, not swapping the system of record.
    3. "What is your cache hit rate today?"
      One question that tells their team you know where the number lives. If the answer is "we don't cache", you have just found the gap.
    4. "How fresh does this screen actually need to be?"
      Turns TTL from a technical setting into a business decision the customer owns. Product catalogue: an hour is fine. Cash position: seconds.
    5. "Caching buys you time before you pay for a bigger database licence."
      The CFO line. Fewer queries reaching the database defers the upgrade — that is real money on a renewal, not a spec-sheet benefit.
