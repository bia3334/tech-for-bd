01Understand

The vault and the cash drawer

A bank's mobile app crawls at the evening peak. The core banking vendor has quoted a throughput licence upgrade. The CTO is annoyed, the CFO has seen the number, and you are in the room.

Here is what is actually happening. Every time somebody opens the app home screen, it asks the core the same handful of questions: what is my balance, what were my last transactions, is my card active, do I have offers. Most of those people close the app four seconds later without doing anything. They were looking, not transacting — but the core was charged for the visit either way.

Tiếng ViệtCore banking (T24, Flexcube…) là hệ thống ghi nhận sự thật về tiền. Nó được thiết kế cho giao dịch chi nhánh và batch cuối ngày — không phải cho vài trăm nghìn người kéo-làm-mới màn hình app lúc 8 giờ tối.

The core is the vault. Redis is the teller's cash drawer.

Nobody opens the vault to hand over five hundred thousand đồng. The teller keeps a drawer for the routine work of the day, and the vault stays the place where the truth lives. Redis is that drawer — an in-memory store sitting in front of the core, answering the repeated, read-only questions in about one millisecond instead of a hundred and forty.

Memory is roughly a hundred times faster than disk. That is the whole mechanism. It is also why Redis is sized in gigabytes rather than terabytes, and why it never becomes the bank's system of record.

The line you never cross

Cache the balance you display. Never cache the balance you authorise against.

Showing a number that is two seconds old is a display problem. Approving a debit against a number that is two seconds old is a loss, a reconciliation break, and a conversation with the regulator. Every risk officer you meet will test you on exactly this boundary — and most vendors fail it by being vague.

Redis in a bank is not only about speed

This is what raises you from "the fast thing" to someone the architects take seriously. Most of what Redis holds in a bank is short-lived state that expires by itself.

  • Session & token storeEvery logged-in user. Auto-logout after 15 minutes of inactivity is a TTL, nothing more.
  • OTP validity"This code expires in 60 seconds" is literally a Redis key with a 60-second TTL.
  • Anti-brute-force"Five wrong PINs and the account locks for 15 minutes" is a counter with a TTL, checked on every attempt.
  • Idempotency keysA customer taps Transfer twice on weak 4G. The duplicate is recognised and ignored — so the money leaves once.
  • Fraud velocity checks"How many transfers has this account made in the last 60 seconds?" — needed in under a millisecond, inside the payment path.
  • FX & interest ratesRead by everyone, changed by almost no one. The textbook cache.
Cache hit / miss
Hit: the answer was already in the drawer. Miss: go to the core, then keep a copy.Đã có sẵn / phải đi lấy rồi ghi lại.
TTL — time to live
How long a value survives before it deletes itself. OTP, session and account lock are all TTLs.Thời hạn sống. Quá dài = số liệu cũ; quá ngắn = lại phải hỏi core.
System of record
The one system that decides what is true. In a bank that is the core — audited, reconciled, never the cache.Nguồn sự thật duy nhất. Cache không bao giờ là nguồn này.
Cache invalidation
Throwing the copy away the moment the real value changes — for example the second a transaction posts.Xoá bản sao ngay khi giá trị thật thay đổi.
Idempotency key
A unique tag on a request so that sending it twice still executes it once.Bấm hai lần vẫn chỉ trừ tiền một lần.
Hit rate
Share of requests answered without touching the core. The single number to ask their architect for.Con số nên hỏi kiến trúc sư của khách.

    05Take away

    Five lines worth stealing

    1. "How much of your evening peak is people looking, not people paying?"
      Splits one vague performance complaint into two different deals with two different price tags. Read traffic is a cache problem. Transaction traffic is a core problem. Most vendors quote the expensive one by default.
    2. "Redis does not replace your core. It stops your core being asked the same question two million times a day."
      Protects their existing T24 or Flexcube investment instead of attacking it. You are adding a layer in front, not proposing a migration nobody asked for.
    3. "Nothing in the authorisation path reads from cache. Display and debit are different risks."
      The sentence that gets you past the risk committee. Say it before they ask, not after.
    4. "How do you handle a double tap on a bad connection today?"
      Idempotency. Double-debit is a headline risk at any retail bank, and almost nobody in a sales meeting asks about it. Ask it and you stop being a vendor.
    5. "If it holds sessions and OTPs, it holds personal data — let's put that on the agenda now, not in the security review."
      A cache full of phone numbers and session tokens is in scope for Nghị định 13/2023 and the bank's own security assessment. Raising it early is worth more than answering it late.
