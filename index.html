Why does the banking app freeze at 8pm?

Every evening, a few hundred thousand people open your customer's app to look at one number. Almost none of them are moving money. All of them are making the bank's most expensive system do work it was never built for.

One question sent and one answer returned is called a . Opening a bank app home screen usually takes six to ten of them before anything appears on screen — balance, recent transactions, card status, offers. The waiting time on each one is called , and it is measured in milliseconds: a thousand milliseconds is one second.

So when a customer says "the app is slow", what they mean is: the questions are taking too long, and there are too many of them.

02The idea

The vault and the cash drawer

Here is the part that matters commercially. At 8pm, most of those hundreds of thousands of people are asking the same questions they asked yesterday, and the core answers every single one from scratch. Worse: many contracts charge the bank based on how much work the core does, so every one of those repeat questions has a price on it.

Most of those people then close the app four seconds later without doing anything at all. They were looking, not paying. The bank was billed either way.

The core is the vault. The cache is the teller's cash drawer.

Nobody opens the vault to hand over five hundred thousand đồng. The teller keeps a drawer for the routine work of the day, and the vault stays the place where the truth lives.

That drawer is a — a copy of an answer, kept somewhere much closer and much faster, so the same question does not have to travel all the way to the vault again. The product almost every bank uses to do this is called .

Why is it so much faster? Because it keeps those copies in the computer's short-term rather than on a disk. Roughly a hundred times quicker to read — but small, expensive, and wiped if the machine restarts. Which tells you immediately what it is not: it is not where a bank keeps its records.

Two words you will hear in every meeting about this: when the answer was already in the drawer, that is a . When it was not there and someone had to walk to the vault, that is a cache miss.

Tiếng ViệtCore = két sắt, giữ sự thật, chậm và tốn tiền mỗi lần mở. Cache = ngăn kéo của giao dịch viên, giữ bản sao câu trả lời, lấy ra trong tích tắc. Redis là sản phẩm làm cái ngăn kéo đó.

03The danger

Where a copy becomes a problem

A copy has one obvious weakness: the moment the real number changes, the copy is wrong. Money lands in the account, the vault knows, the drawer does not — and the customer opens the app and sees the old figure.

Banks control this in two ways, and both have names you should recognise. First, every copy gets an expiry stamp: keep this for thirty seconds, then delete yourself. That stamp is called , and the same mechanism is what makes an OTP code expire, what logs you out after fifteen idle minutes, and what unlocks an account fifteen minutes after too many wrong PINs. Second, the moment a transaction posts, the bank deliberately throws the copy away — that is called it.

The line you never cross

Copy the balance you show. Never copy the balance you approve a payment against.

A number on screen being two seconds old is a display problem. Letting money leave an account based on a number that is two seconds old is a loss, a broken reconciliation, and a conversation with the regulator. Everything inside the reads from the vault, always. Every risk officer you meet will test you on exactly this — and most salespeople fail it by being vague.

What else lives in that drawer

This is the part that moves you from "the person selling speed" to someone the bank's architects take seriously. Most of what Redis holds in a bank is not a balance at all — it is short-lived information that deletes itself.

  • Who is currently logged inYour . Kept for fifteen minutes of inactivity, then gone — which is why the app logs you out.
  • "This code expires in 60 seconds"An OTP is a value with a sixty-second expiry stamp on it. That is the whole implementation.
  • "Five wrong PINs and the account locks for 15 minutes"A counter that erases itself after fifteen minutes. Checked on every attempt, so it has to be instant.
  • Stopping a double paymentA customer taps Transfer, the app seems frozen on weak 4G, they tap again. The bank attaches a ticket number to each instruction and remembers it for a few minutes — same ticket, same instruction, money leaves once. That ticket number is called an .
  • Fraud checks in the middle of a payment"How many transfers has this account made in the last sixty seconds?" — needed in under a millisecond, while the customer is waiting.
  • Exchange ratesRead by everybody, changed by almost nobody. The textbook case for a copy.

    07Take away

    Five lines worth stealing

    1. "How much of your evening peak is people looking, not people paying?"
      Splits one vague complaint into two different problems with two very different price tags. People looking is a cache problem. People paying is a core problem. Most vendors quote the expensive one by default.
    2. "We are not replacing your core. We are stopping it from being asked the same question two million times a day."
      Protects the decision they already made and paid for, instead of attacking it. You are adding a layer in front, not proposing a migration nobody asked for.
    3. "Nothing in the payment approval reads from a copy. What we show and what we approve are two different risks."
      The sentence that gets you past the risk committee. Say it before they ask, not after.
    4. "How do you handle a customer tapping Transfer twice on a bad connection today?"
      Money leaving twice is a headline risk at any retail bank, and almost nobody in a sales meeting asks about it. Ask it and you stop being a vendor.
    5. "If it holds login sessions and OTP codes, it holds personal data — let's put that on the agenda now, not in the security review."
      Phone numbers, session records and OTPs are personal data under Nghị định 13/2023, and the bank's security assessment will ask where those servers physically sit. Raising it yourself is worth more than answering it late.
